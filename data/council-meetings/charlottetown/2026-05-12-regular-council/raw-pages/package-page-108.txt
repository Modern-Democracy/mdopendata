6) Reports/Discussions
 a. Introduction to the Charlottetown Area Watershed Association - ES 26-06

 Alistair Ozon delivered a presentation introducing the Charlottetown Area Watershed
 Association (CAWA), a newly rebranded and expanded organization formed through the
 amalgamation of the former Ellen's Creek Watershed Group and the Wrights Creek
 Environmental Committee. The purpose of the report was to inform Council of this transition and
 to seek approval to consolidate two previously separate grants of $10,000 into a single $20,000
 grant allocated to CAWA. It was clarified that this change would not increase the City's financial
 contribution as the $20,000 has been approved through the 26/27 operating budget.
 Consolidating the grant funding will streamline administration and reflect the new organizational
 structure.

 Alistair Ozon outlined the benefits of supporting CAWA, noting that the organization enhances
 the City's capacity to undertake environmental initiatives such as tree planting, invasive species
 removal, and watershed restoration. The group provides access to lower-cost plant materials
 through the provincial nursery, mobilizes volunteers, facilitates engagement with private
 landowners, and allows the City to access grant funding opportunities that it could not pursue
 independently. The partnership also aligns with the City's Climate Action Plan, particularly in
 strengthening watershed management and ecological protection.

 Highlights from the 2025 field season were presented, including extensive tree planting across
 multiple parks, removal of approximately 4,300 kilograms of invasive species, and the collection
 of over 100 kilograms of waste from waterways. The organization also contributed to educational
 programming and community engagement. Councillor Tweel raised concerns regarding a
 perceived imbalance in resources between Wrights Creek and Ellen's Creek. Alistair Ozon
 responded that differences are largely due to land ownership constraints and site characteristics,
 noting that Ellen's Creek includes more private land, which limits opportunities. He emphasized
 that funding levels have been consistent between watersheds and that the newly unified
 organization intends to allocate resources equitably through improved planning processes.

 Following discussion, a motion was moved by Councillor Beck and seconded by Councillor Tweel
 to recommend approval of the grant consolidation, and the motion carried.
