Shaw and Stuart Dodson described a model that uses distributed battery systems installed in
 homes and businesses, which are then connected through centralized software to function
 collectively as a virtual power plant. This approach was presented as a way to improve grid
 efficiency, manage peak demand, and support the integration of renewable energy sources such
 as wind and solar.

 The presenters highlighted several potential benefits, including reduced reliance on large-scale
 infrastructure investments, improved energy resilience, and opportunities for residents to play
 a more active role in energy management. The model was described as scalable and adaptable
 to the needs of Prince Edward Island, with the potential to be implemented incrementally over
 time. Discussion included questions related to cost, with estimates in the range of $30,000 per
 household prior to available incentives, as well as the role of federal programs that could help
 offset these costs. Councillor MacKinnon also asked about performance in colder climates,
 with presenters noting that while battery efficiency can be affected by low temperatures,
 systems remain functional and can be installed in protected environments such as garages or
 indoor spaces.

 The role of Maritime Electric was also discussed, with presenters noting that while collaboration
 with the utility would be beneficial, the model can initially operate independently through
 behind-the-meter systems, with potential for future integration. Mayor Brown also reflected
 more broadly on the City's role in energy planning and the potential for regional collaboration
 with neighboring municipalities.

 The presentation was received for information, with no motion brought forward.
