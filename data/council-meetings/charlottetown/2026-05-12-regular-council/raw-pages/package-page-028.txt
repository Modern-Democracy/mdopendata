Request:
This is a request for Major Variances to allow for a maximum of up to 64 apartment units for
property identified as 15 Clonhaven Street whereas a maximum of 44 apartment units are
otherwise permitted, a maximum of up to 64 apartment units for property identified as 35
Clonhaven Street whereas a maximum of 48 apartment units are otherwise permitted and for a
maximum of up to 64 apartment units for property identified as 165 Carraigbrook Avenue whereas
a maximum of 59 apartment units are otherwise permitted. All three properties abut one other in
the (R-4) Zone and form part of the same PID# 1047562.
Summary:

David Gundrum stated that the proposal is for a major variance application concerning the
properties at 15 Clonhaven Street, 35 Clonhaven Street, and 165 Carraigbrook Avenue (PID#
1047562), requesting increased apartment density. The development proposes 64 apartment
units on each of the three lots, whereas the current permitted densities would allow for 44, 48,
and 59 units respectively. The three properties are currently vacant and located within a recently
approved greenfield subdivision, bounded by Clonhaven Street, Carraigbrook Avenue, and
Malpeque Road, with surrounding lands consisting of similar higher-density zoning and nearby
lower-density residential development. The proposal includes six 3-storey apartment buildings,
each containing 32 units, for a combined total of 192 units across all lots, compared to 151 units
otherwise permitted, representing an overall increase of 27%. Notice of the application was
circulated to 28 property owners within 100 metres, with one letter received expressing
concerns regarding the scale of the proposed development. It was stated that the analysis of the
application indicates that sufficient land exists to accommodate parking and that the proposed
form of development is consistent with the intended use of the area, though it does not meet
minimum lot area requirements associated with maximum density. Based on this review, it was
recommended that the Planning Board support the approval of the requested major variances to
allow up to 64 units on each property.

Discussion:

It was asked why the three properties, although all zoned R-4, have different maximum permitted
unit counts and whether this was based on lot size calculations. It was explained that the variation
is due to minimum lot area per unit requirements, noting that each lot has a different size and
configuration, including one being a corner lot, which results in differing allowable densities.

It was further asked whether the subject lands had previously been considered for a larger-scale
development. It was confirmed that a prior concept included approximately 10 apartment
buildings of up to six storeys with significantly higher unit counts, and that this concept formed
the basis of the earlier rezoning of the lands.

It was stated that since that time, the lands have been further subdivided, resulting in a
different lot layout and development configuration, and that the current proposal reflects those
changes. It was explained that subdivision does not require a public consultation process and is
handled administratively, and that changes to layout and design can occur following rezonings
based on various factors including market conditions.
