Discussion:

It was asked whether the newly constructed building on Weymouth
Street, located between neighbouring properties, was currently occupied and where it fit within
the housing continuum. It was stated that staff were not aware if the building was occupied at
that time. It was explained that the development would generally be categorized as supportive
housing, noting that it includes some level of on-site staffing, although it may not strictly align
with a transitional housing model.

It was noted that supportive housing typically includes services such as counselling and needs
assessments, and it was confirmed that this would be consistent with the type of development
referenced. It was further observed that the building integrates well into the
surrounding neighbourhood and aligns with the housing spectrum outlined in the Official Plan. It
was stated that when considering proposed zoning changes, there is a need to balance
community acceptance with broader housing needs. It was noted that concerns had been raised
by nearby residents regarding safety and compatibility, and that additional measures to improve
community confidence had been discussed, including surveillance and engagement initiatives. It
was expressed that while there is general support for transitional and supportive housing, there
must be efforts to address public concerns through meaningful mitigation measures. It was
questioned whether sufficient steps had been taken by the applicant to engage with the
community prior to the application proceeding. It was explained that certain proposed safety
measures, including surveillance systems, were identified; however, limitations were noted in
what the applicant was prepared to implement. It was further stated that additional measures
could be formalized through a Development Agreement to ensure accountability. It was clarified
that site-specific zoning approvals are permanent in nature and cannot be issued on a temporary
or trial basis, noting that such approvals are either granted or denied. It was also noted that while
public safety considerations may be relevant, they are generally supplementary to the land-use
compatibility analysis undertaken by Planning staff, with Council ultimately responsible for
