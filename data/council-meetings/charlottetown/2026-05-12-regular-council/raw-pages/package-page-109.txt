The Bike Bus program was highlighted as a growing initiative, promoting active transportation
among students, with participation from five schools and approximately 150 students expressing
interest. Sydney Gillis noted that expansion is dependent on volunteer capacity and school
engagement, with some barriers identified due to student transportation patterns and school
resources.

Educational initiatives such as Gardening in the Classroom will engage over 200 students in
learning about food production and sustainability through hands-on activities. Additional events
include a landscaping workshop aimed at promoting environmentally friendly lawn care
practices, and a Fix-It Fair in partnership with the Charlottetown Library to encourage repair and
reuse of household items. Gardening in Small Spaces will offer accessible gardening education
for residents, while the annual Lady Slipper Hike will provide guided ecological learning
opportunities in partnership with watershed organizations.

Arbor Day celebrations will continue as a long-standing tradition involving local grade 5 and 6
students in tree planting and environmental education, and the Business Tree Planting Challenge
will engage local businesses in offsetting paper use through tree planting initiatives. A new Local
Food Discovery Week was introduced as a pilot project designed to promote locally sourced food
through a passport-style program encouraging residents to visit participating vendors.
Community planting events will also continue throughout June, with a target of engaging 150
participants and planting approximately 600 native species. These efforts contribute to the City's
broader urban forestry recovery strategy following Hurricane Fiona.

Funding for these initiatives is primarily drawn from the 2026-27 operating budget, with
supplementary support from external partners, including a recently secured TDFEF grant for an
edible planting project at Johnston Park. Mayor Brown expressed support for the breadth of
programming and its alignment with sustainability goals.

c. Transit Service Agreement - ES 26-08

Jessika Corkum-Gorrill shared an update regarding the status of the transit service agreement
negotiations. It was reported that while substantial progress has been made, the agreement is
not yet ready for presentation to Council due to ongoing legal review and refinement of contract
language. Jessika Corkum-Gorrill emphasized that the agreement is complex and long-term in
nature, making it critical that all terms are carefully reviewed and finalized to ensure clarity and
accuracy.

She requested approval to extend the existing temporary "bridge rate" of $78 per hour for an
additional one-month period beyond March 31, 2026. This rate applies specifically to expanded
transit services introduced in 2025 and was originally approved to account for service delivery
in lower-density areas where farebox revenue alone would not be sufficient. The extension is
intended to maintain uninterrupted service while negotiations continue.
