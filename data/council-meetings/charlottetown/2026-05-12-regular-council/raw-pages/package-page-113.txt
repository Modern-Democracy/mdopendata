c. 2025 Transit Service Expansions Hourly Rate - ES 26-12

Jessika Corkum-Gorrill presented a report highlighting a request from the City's transit operator,
Trius, to increase the hourly rate for transit service expansions delivered in 2025. She explained
that while an hourly rate of $78 had previously been agreed upon, the operator is now requesting
an interim increase to $89 per hour until a new service agreement is finalized. The request was
based on the operator's experience with newly expanded routes, particularly in areas with lower
ridership, where fare revenue is not sufficient to offset the operating costs of those routes if they
are considered in isolation from the overall system. In addition, the operator noted that the
updated service agreement, which had been expected to be in place by April 1, has not yet been
finalized, and the requested increase would serve as a temporary measure in the meantime.

Jessika Corkum Gorrill advised that the financial impact of the increase would be approximately
$2,400 per month. Committee members engaged in a detailed discussion, Mayor Brown raised
concerns about the evolving nature of the transit model, including increasing costs, ongoing
adjustments, and the financial sustainability of expanding service into lower ridership areas.
Councillor MacKinnon also raised a question about what would occur if the increase was not
approved. Jessika Corkum-Gorrill indicated that the operator remains under contract to provide
the service, but the matter would need to be referred back to the operator and CATCC for
consideration.

The discussion also touched on broader transit issues, including the challenges of balancing
service expansion with financial realities and the importance of finalizing a comprehensive
service agreement to provide greater stability moving forward. Jessika Corkum-Gorrill noted that
the upcoming agreement is intended to address these concerns and provide a clearer framework
for service delivery.

A motion was then brought forward to approve the interim rate increase. The motion generated
mixed responses among members, with Councillor Tweel expressing opposition. Following
further discussion, the vote resulted in a tie, with the Mayor in favour and Councillor Tweel
opposed. The Chair exercised the tie-breaking vote in favour of the motion, allowing it to proceed
to Council for further consideration. Moved by Mayor, seconded, and carried by tie-breaking vote
of the Chair. Carried.
