     Growing Demand for Municipal Services
     Population growth and regional service demand have also contributed to increased use of
     municipal services.

     Public transit usage has grown significantly since the service was launched in 2015. In 2025, T3
     Transit recorded 1.4 million rides, compared with 403,683 rides in its first year of operation. This
     growth reflects the increasing importance of transit in supporting mobility within the city and
     across the Capital Region.

     Emergency services have also experienced rising demand. Between 2020 and 2024:

         Calls for service to Charlottetown Police Services increased by 35%
         Charlottetown Fire Department calls increased by 42.8%

     These increases reflect both population growth and the city’s role as the primary service centre for
     much of the Island.

     Infrastructure Pressures
     Like many municipalities across Atlantic Canada, Charlottetown’s core infrastructure—including
     water and wastewater systems—was originally constructed to serve a much smaller population
     than the city supports today.

     Maintaining, upgrading and expanding this infrastructure to meet current and future demand
     represents one of the city’s most significant long-term financial responsibilities.


 Moving Toward Sustainable Funding Solutions
     These challenges are not unique to Charlottetown but are common among capital cities and
     regional service hubs. Recognizing this, the City of Charlottetown continues to work with the
     Federation of Prince Edward Island Municipalities and other local governments in advocating for
     a more equitable, long-term funding framework that reflects the realities of modern municipal
     service delivery.

     Strengthening collaboration with provincial and federal partners will be essential to ensuring that
     Charlottetown can continue to provide the infrastructure and services required to support
     residents, businesses, and visitors—while sustaining the city’s role as the capital and economic
     centre of PEI.
