     Funding & Taxation
     Sources of Funding
     The City receives funding for services and capital projects from the following sources:
        Property taxes
        Provincial/federal grants
        Water and Sewer service fees
        User fees


     Tax Collection & Revenue Sharing
     How Property Taxes Work in Charlottetown
     Property taxes for Charlottetown are collected and administered by the Government of Prince
     Edward Island. The province is responsible for billing and collecting municipal property taxes,
     provincial property taxes, provincial income taxes, as well as fire and waste management
     charges. The municipal portion of property tax revenue is then remitted to the City on a monthly
     basis. These municipal revenues support core local services, including policing, fire protection,
     public transit, and infrastructure maintenance and renewal.

     Water and Sewer utility fees are collected and managed directly by the City and are funded
     separately from property taxes.
     Assessment
     Property assessments are conducted annually by the province. Assessment notices and property
     tax bills are typically issued in early May.

     Rate Setting
     Each March, City Council establishes the municipal property tax rate as part of the annual
     budgeting process. The current municipal residential rate is $0.67 per $100 of assessed value. This
     rate is applied in addition to the provincial rate of $1.50 per $100 of assessed value, which
     includes a $0.50 residential credit.
     Revenue Sharing with the Province
     Under the current structure, the province collects all property taxes and remits the municipal
     portion to the City. Across PEI, municipalities — including Charlottetown — receive approximately
     2.2 cents of every property tax dollar collected. By comparison, municipalities across Canada
     typically receive between 8 and 10 cents per property tax dollar collected. In addition to property
     tax revenues, Charlottetown receives provincial transfers that help support important services
     and programs that residents rely on.

     The City continues to engage with the Province on revenue-sharing arrangements, with the goal
     of achieving a more equitable and sustainable revenue-sharing model to help the city address
     increased infrastructure demands, public safety costs, and population growth pressures.
