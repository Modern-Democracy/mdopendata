Capital City Context and Service Pressures
As the provincial capital and largest municipality in Prince Edward Island, Charlottetown plays a
central role in the Island’s economic, administrative, and cultural life. This role brings unique
opportunities, but also distinct service and infrastructure challenges.

Historic Population Growth
Charlottetown’s population is approximately 48,700 residents. However, the broader Capital
Region, which includes the neighbouring towns of Stratford and Cornwall and surrounding rural
areas, has a population of roughly 96,500 people and accounts for more than half of PEI’s total
population. As the Island’s primary employment, education, government, and service centre,
Charlottetown supports not only its residents but also a much larger regional population that rely
on the city’s infrastructure and municipal services each day.

Daytime Population and Regional Service Demand
Charlottetown experiences a significant daily influx of workers, students, and visitors. While the
city’s infrastructure is funded primarily through municipal property taxes paid by Charlottetown
residents and businesses, it serves a population that extends well beyond municipal boundaries.

Traffic data illustrates the scale of this regional demand. In 2023, the average daily traffic volume
at the five major access points into Charlottetown totaled approximately 107,414 vehicles per day.
The Capital Region population is nearly double that of the city proper, indicating the significant
number of commuters and service users who rely on Charlottetown’s road network, transit system,
public spaces, and emergency services.

Many of these users reside outside city limits and do not contribute to municipal revenues through
property taxes. At the same time, the costs associated with maintaining and expanding
infrastructure have increased significantly, while municipal revenue tools have not evolved at the
same pace as service demands.

Tourism and Seasonal Population Growth
Charlottetown also serves as one of PEI’s primary tourism gateways. The city welcomes visitors by
air, land, and sea, and is home to one of Atlantic Canada’s busiest cruise ports.

The Port of Charlottetown expects nearly 80 cruise ship calls in 2026, bringing an estimated
+165,000 visitors to the city. This influx contributes to the local economy and supports businesses,
cultural attractions, and hospitality services. At the same time, it places additional seasonal
demand on municipal infrastructure and services, including policing and emergency response,
water and wastewater systems, traffic management, and the maintenance of parks, streets, and
public spaces.
