## Source Claims

Chapter 6 separates service provision decisions from service production decisions. Provision concerns whether a service should exist, how much should be spent, and how it should be financed. Production concerns how the service is delivered, including own-forces delivery, contracting, joint production, corporations, public-private partnerships, franchising, vouchers, volunteer production, and co-production.

The chapter frames service-production fit around economies of scope, economies of scale, and four service characteristics: measurability of inputs and results, labour or capital intensity, face-to-face interaction, and continuous or occasional production.

The chapter treats performance measurement as a management and accountability practice built from inputs, processes, outputs, and outcomes. It distinguishes routine performance measures from broader program evaluation and benefit-cost analysis.

The chapter warns against single-measure evaluation. Performance comparison should use multiple measures and account for background or environmental variables.

The chapter identifies longitudinal comparison, benchmarking against stated objectives, cross-sectional comparison among similar governments, program evaluation, benefit-cost analysis, sustainability measures, and activity-based budgeting as distinct analysis modes.

## Charlottetown Prototype Implications

Chapter 6 supports a municipal portal workflow that first inventories services, producers, objectives, measures, source documents, and cost records before calculating performance comparisons.

For Charlottetown, the immediate prototype should focus on source availability:

- Which services have public objectives or stated standards.
- Which services publish recurring performance measures.
- Which services have activity-level cost data versus department-level line-item budget data.
- Which services are own-forces, contracted, jointly produced, volunteer-produced, or delivered through another arrangement.
- Which measures can be compared over time without changing definitions.

The chapter does not justify a public ranking model by itself. Any cross-municipality comparison requires standardized measures and comparable environmental context.

## Required Data Inventory

| Data class | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Service catalogue | Identifies municipal services and responsible departments or producers. |
| Production arrangement records | Supports own-forces, contracted, joint, corporation, partnership, franchise, voucher, volunteer, and co-production classification. |
| Annual reports and objectives | Links performance measures to stated municipal objectives. |
| Budget and activity-cost records | Supports cost per input, process, output, or outcome analysis. |
| Service output records | Supports trend, benchmark, and cross-sectional comparison. |
| Outcome or satisfaction evidence | Supports program evaluation beyond agency output statistics. |
| Environmental/context variables | Controls for factors outside program control when comparing results. |
| Contracts and service standards | Supports production-fit review and benchmark against stated objective. |

## Review Limits

Chapter 6 is BC-specific and references BC statutory reporting context as of 2008. Current Charlottetown or PEI obligations require separate legal and source review.

The chapter provides evaluation concepts and process candidates, but it does not provide enough detail to define final portal schemas, scoring methods, or public performance grades.
